RUNNING HEADS: דחה־די | CXCV | דחה־די [?]

דָּחָה TO THRUST, TO PUSH, TO THROW down, stoßen, umstoßen. (Arabic دحا id., see Jeuhari in Schultens, on Job p. 1101; also *de coitu*, like ضرب and other verbs of thrusting, see דַּחֲוָה. Syriac and Ch. [Syriac], דְּחָא id. The idea of thrusting, pushing, knocking, impelling, is found in many verbs, whose primary syllable is דח, as נָדַח, דָּחַק, דָּחַף, דָּחַח, דּוּחַ, דָּחַם, [Syriac], [Arabic], [Arabic], [Arabic], compare similar families under the words דָּכַךְ and דָּקַק.) Ps. 35:5; 118:13, דָּחֹה דְחִיתַנִי לִנְפֹּל " thou hast thrust at me that I might fall;" 140:5. Ps. 62:4, גָּדֵר הַדְּחוּיָה " an overturned wall."
NIPHAL, pass. of Kal *to be thrust away*, Pro. 14:32, " the wicked is driven away in his wickedness," i. e. perishes, rushes to destruction. Compare יַדְּחוּ (prop. from דָּחַח), Jer. 23:12. But the part. plur. constr. נִדְחֵי, as נִדְחֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, is more correctly referred to נָדַח which see.
PUAL, pret. דֹּחוּ " they are thrown down," Ps. 36:13.
Derivatives מִדְחֶה, דְּחִי and—

דַּחֲוָה f. pl. דַּחֲוָן, Ch. *a concubine* (from the root דְּחָה, Arabic دحا and نحا subegit feminam). Dan. 6:19, וְדַחֲוָן לָא־הַנְעֵל קָדָמוֹהִי " nor did he allow concubines to be brought in to him." Theodot. and the Syriac arbitrarily interpret it *food;* the Hebrew interpreters better, " musical instruments," especially such as were struck.

דָּחַח i. q. דָּחָה whence fut. Niph. יִדַּחוּ [?] Jer. 23:12. But if written יַדְּחוּ [?] it may be referred to דָּחָה.

דְּחִי in pause דֶּחִי m. (from the root דָּחָה) *a thrusting* down, *overthrowing*, Ps. 56:14; 116:8.

דְּחַל Ch. *to fear*, i. q. Hebr. זָחַל prop. to creep along, to go with a quiet gait, like timid persons, furchtsam heranschleichen. To this corresponds the Syr. [Syriac] to fear. Arab. دحل to flee, to withdraw, pr. sich davonschleichen, to withdraw oneself secretly. Constr. followed by מִן קֳדָם (compare יָרֵא מִפְּנֵי), Dan. 5:19. Part. דְּחִיל terrible, Dan. 2:31; 7:7.
PAEL דַּחֵל *to make afraid, terrify*. Dan. 4:2.

דָּחַן an unused root, Arabic دخن *to smoke*, hence used of a smoky, dusky colour; whence apparently—

דֹּחַן m. Arabic دخن Ezekiel 4:9, *millet* (*holcus dochna*, Linn.), Germ. Meerhirsen, a kind of corn, of

דָּחַף TO THRUST, TO IMPEL, TO URGE, see the root דָּחָה. Part. pass. *impelled*, hastened, urged on. Est. 3:15; 8:14.
NIPHAL נִדְחַף *to impel oneself, to hasten*. 2 Ch. 26:20; Est. 6:12. Hence מַדְחֵפוֹת.

דָּחַק TO THRUST, TO PUSH, as is done in a great crowd, Joel 2:8 (Arabic دحق to repel, to drive away, دحيق cast aside, whence the quadriliteral دحقب to push from behind, compounded of دحق and عقب. Aram. [Syriac], דְּחַק i. q. Heb. With this accords the Gr. διώκω). Part. דֹּחֵק an oppressor (of a people). Jud. 2:18.

דַּי const. state דֵּי, suffix דַּיְי [?]—(1) subst. *sufficiency, a large enough quantity*, hence adverb, enough. The form is as if from the verb דָּיָה = דָּיַי [?] (like חַי, حى from حى), which, according to Simonis, has the same meaning as ادى to be many. It may also be said that דַּי is put by aphæresis for אֲדַי, of the form לְשַׁדִּי, לְשַׁד; זְמַנִּי, זְמַן [?].—Esther 1:18, וּכְדַי בִּזָּיוֹן וָקָצֶף " and there will be enough of contempt and anger." Mal. 3:10, " I will pour you out a blessing עַד בְּלִי דָי until (there is) not sufficiency," until all my abundance be exhausted, and as this never can be, it means, for ever; comp. Ps. 72:6. (Jo. Simonis renders it well as to the sense, *ultra quam satis est*, but how he draws this from the words I cannot at all see.) The genitive which follows this word, commonly signifies the thing or person *for whom* something suffices. Prov. 25:16, דַּיֶּךָּ " which is sufficient for thee." Ex. 36:7, דַּיָּם " sufficient for them." Obad. 5; Jer. 49:9. Lev. 5:7, דֵּי שֶׂה " enough for (i. e. to buy) a lamb" (not as given by Simonis ed. 1—4: so many persons as were enough to eat a lamb). Lev. 12:8; 25:26, כְּדֵי גְאֻלָּתוֹ " enough to redeem him." Neh. 5:8, כְּדֵי בָנוּ " so far as was in us," according to our power. The genitive more rarely signifies that of which there is enough.
