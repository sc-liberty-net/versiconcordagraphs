PIEL—(1) *to bind up* (wounds), followed by לְ of pers. Ps. 147:3.

(2) *to bind fast, to restrain*, Job 28:11, מִבְּכִי נְהָרוֹת חִבֵּשׁ "he stops up the streams that they do not trickle;" spoken of a miner stopping off the water from flowing into his pits.

PUAL, *to be bound up* (as a wound), Isa. 1:6; Eze. 30:21.

חָבַת an unused root, prob. TO COOK, TO BAKE bread. Æth. ኅብስት: Arabic خُبْز bread, خبز to bake bread. Hence מַחֲבַת a cooking pan, and—

חֲבִתִּים m. plur. *things cooked*, or *baked pastry*, 1 Ch. 9:31; compare מַחֲבַת.

חַג constr. and followed by לְ (Ex. 12:14; Num. 29:12) חַג, with suff. חַגִּי m.

(1) *a festival* (from the root חָגַג), Ex. 10:9; 12:14. חַג חָג, עָשָׂה חָג to keep a festival Levit. 23:39; Deut. 16:10. In the Talmud κατ' ἐξοχήν, it is used of the feast of tabernacles, and so 2 Ch. 5:3; comp. 1 Ki. 8:2. ["So of the passover, Isa. 30:29. Comp. Arabic حَجّ pilgrimage to Mecca."]

(2) meton. *a festival sacrifice, a victim*, Ps. 118:27, אִסְרוּ־חַג בַּעֲבֹתִים "bind the sacrifice with cords." Ex. 23:18, חֵלֶב חַגִּי "the fat of my sacrifice;" Mal. 2:3. Compare מוֹעֵד 2 Ch. 30:22.

חָגָא i. q. חָגָּה (which is the reading of many copies) f. *fear, terror*, Isa. 19:17. Root חָגַג No. 3.

חָגַב an unused root. Arab. حجب to hide, to veil. Hence—

חָגָב m.—(1) *a locust*, winged and edible (Lev. 11:22), said to be so called because it covers the ground, Nu. 13:33; Isa. 40:22; Ecc. 12:5. ["Another etymology is proposed by Credner, on Joel page 309. The Samar. in Lev. loc. cit. has חרנבה which may signify *a leaper*, compare Arab. حرجل; and from חרנב then might come the triliteral חָגָב; comp. חָגַל, حرجل."]

(2) [*Hagab*], pr. n. m., Ezr. 2:46.

חֲגָבָה ("locust"), [*Hagaba*], pr. n. m., Ezr. 2:45 [א׳]; Neh. 7:48.

חָגַג (kindred to the root חוּג) to go round in a circle, hence—

(1) *to dance*, 1 Sa. 30:16.

=== RIGHT COLUMN ===

(2) *to keep a festival*, from the idea of leaping and dancing in sacred dances, Ex. 5:1; Lev. 23 41; especially of a public assembly, Psal. 42:5 (Syriac [Syriac], [Syriac] id. Arabic حج to go to Mecca, as a *Hadj* or pilgrim, to keep the public festival.)

(3) *to reel, to be giddy*, used of drunkards, Ps. 107:27; applied to a person terrified, whence חָגָא fear. Hence are derived חַג, חָגָא, and the proper names חַגַּי, חַגִּי, חַגָּיָה, חַגִּית.

חָגָה an unused root, i. q. Arabic حجا to take refuge with some one, whence—

חֲגָוִים m. pl. חַגְוֵי־הַסֶּלַע *places of refuge in the rocks* [perhaps *dwellings carved in the rocks*], Cant. 2:14; Obad. 3; Jer. 49:16. (Arabic مَحْجَأة[?] a refuge, an asylum. Syr. [Syriac], [Syriac] a lofty rock, a cliff. [It is doubtful whether these Syriac words are really used in these senses.])

חָגוֹר [of the form קְטוֹל], m., pr. verbal adj. from the root חָגַר—(1) *binding*, hence *a girdle, a belt*, 1 Sa. 18:4.

(2) intrans. *girded, clad.* Ezek. 23:15, חֲגוֹרֵי אֵזוֹר "girded with a girdle", compare 2 Ki. 3:21.

חֲגוֹרָה f. *a girdle*, 2 Sam. 18:11 (from the root חָגַר), *an apron*, Gen. 3:7.

חַגַּי ("festive," from חַג with the termination ־ַי i. q. ־ִי) pr. n of *Haggai* the prophet. LXX. Ἀγγαῖος Hag. 1:1.

חַגִּי (id.), [*Haggi*], pr. n. of a son of Gad, Num. 26:15. Patron. is the same [for חַגִּיִּי] ibid.

חַגִּיָּה ("festival of Jehovah"), [*Haggiah*], pr. n. m., 1 Ch. 6:15.

חַגִּית ("festive"), [*Haggith*], pr. n. of a wife of David, the mother of Adonijah, 2 Sam. 3:4; 1 Ki. 1:5.

חָגַל an unused root. Arabic حجل to advance by short leaps in the manner of a crow, or of a man with his feet tied. This triliteral appears to have sprung from the quadriliteral חַרְגֹּל (which see), by omitting ר. Hence—

חָגְלָה ("a partridge"), like Arab. حَجَل, Syr [Syriac], [*Hoglah*], pr. n. fem., Num. 26:33; 27:1; 36:11.
