רְחַבְיָהוּ [and יָ֫ה[?]] ("for whom Jehovah makes an ample space," i. e. whom he makes happy, and sets free), [*Rehabiah*], pr. n. m. 1 Chron. 23:17; 24:21; 26:25.

רְחַבְעָם ("who enlarges the people," compare Ex. 34:24; as if Εὑρύδημος) [*Rehoboam*], pr. n. of the son and successor of Solomon, who governed the kingdom of Judah, 975—58 B. C., 1 Ki. 11:43; 12:1, seqq.; 14:21. LXX. Ῥοβοάμ.

רָחָה an unused root, which appears to have had the signification of *rubbing, crushing* (compare Arab. [Arabic], to tread, to trample, also the syllable רח in the cognate אָרַח to tread a way, רָחַץ, מָרַח). Arab. [Arabic], to construct a mill, to turn a mill, is secondary, and derived from the noun [Arabic]. Hence—

רֵחֶה m. *a millstone*, so called from rubbing and making small; only found in dual רֵחַיִם *handmills*, prop. two stones, Ex. 11:5; Nu. 11:8; Isa. 47:2; Arab. [Arabic], dual [Arabic], id.

רַחוּם m. *merciful*, only used of God, commonly joined with חַנּוּן Deut. 4:31; Psa. 86:15, etc.; from the root רָחַם.

רְחוּם ("beloved" ["merciful"]), [*Rehum*], pr. n. m.—(1) of a Persian governor in Samaria, Ezr. 4:8.—(2) Neh. 3:17.—(3) Ezr. 2:2; Neh. 10:26; for which there is, Neh. 7:7 (prob. by error of a copyist) נְחוּם.—(4) Neh. 12:3; otherwise חָרִם verse 15; 7:42.

רָחוֹק m. [f. רְחוֹקָה] adj. (from the root רָחַק) *far off, remote*.—(a) from a place, Gen. 37:18; Ex. 2:4; and often. Subst. *distance, space*, Josh. 3:4; and with prepp. בְּרָחוֹק at a distance, *afar*, Psalm 10:1; מֵרָחוֹק Gen. 22:4; Isa. 49:1, and לְמֵרָחוֹק Job 36:3; 39:29; *from afar*, but מֵרָחוֹק is also *far* (see מִן No. 3, c). עַד מֵרָחוֹק as far as distant places, Isa. 57:9; Neh. 12:43.—(b) of time, whence מֵרָחוֹק from a long while ago, Isa. 22:11; 25:1; לְמֵרָחוֹק id. Isa. 37:26.—(c) in respect to help, Ps. 10:1; 22:2. Metaph. any thing is called *remote* which we cannot

רָחַט ["an unused and uncertain root; whence"—]

רָחִיט Cant. 1:17 כתיב, i. q. קרי רָהִיט, *laqueare, carved or fretted ceiling*; either an error of a copyist, or else ה in this word was sometimes pronounced more harshly (like ח), as by the Samaritans, who in the Pentateuch for רהטים have רחטים. Ewald on Cant. loc. cit. considers that רָחִיט is put with the letters transposed, for חָרִיט, [Arabic] turned work; but I prefer the previous explanation.

רֵחַיִם dual, *handmills*, see רֵחֶה.

רַחִיק Ch. *far off, remote*, Ezr. 6:6.

רָחֵל an unused root, perhaps of the same or similar meaning to רָחַם to cherish. Secondary and denom. is the Arab. [Arabic], Conj. V. to possess lambs. [In Thes. this is compared with [Arabic], to wander, to journey, especially with camels.]

רָחֵל f.—(1) *a ewe*, Gen. 31:38; 32:15; hence any *sheep*, Isaiah 53:7; Cant. 6:6. (Arab. [Arabic], [Arabic], a female lamb.)
(2) [*Rachel, Rahel*], pr. n. of the wife of Jacob, Gen. 29:6; Jer. 31:15.

רָחַם fut. יִרְחַם ["prop. TO BE SOFT;" hence—] TO LOVE, Ps. 18:2. (Syr. [Syriac], Arab. [Arabic] and [Arabic] id. The primary idea appears to be in cherishing, soothing, and in a gentle emotion of the mind; compare רָחַף.)
PIEL רִחַם, inf. רַחֵם, fut. יְרַחֵם *to behold with tenderest affection, to compassionate*, followed by an acc. Ex. 33:19; Deu. 13:18; 30:3; followed by עַל Psa. 103:13; used of the love of parents towards their children, Psa. loc. cit.; Isa. 49:15; and of the compassion of God towards men, Ps. 116:5.
PUAL רֻחַם *to obtain mercy*, Prov. 28:13; Hosea 14:4; compare 1:6.
Derivatives, רֶחֶם—רַחַם, רַחוּם, רַחֲמָנִי, and pr. n. רָחוּם, לֹא רֻחָמָה, יְרַחְמְאֵל, יְרֹחָם.
