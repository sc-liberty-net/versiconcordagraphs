RUNNING HEADS: פלג־פלט | DCLXXV | פיתון־פלט

*watercourse*, so called from the idea of *dividing*, comp. the verb, Job 38:25. I suspect, however, the root פלג also to have had the meaning of *flowing, fluctuating, bubbling up*; compare *flu-o, fluc-si, fluctus*, φλύ-ω, also πέλαγος, Æth. [Ethiopic]: to bubble, to bubble up; from the biliteral stock פל, compare פּוּל, also *bullire*, wallen, Welle. Psa. 65:10, פֶּלֶג אֱלֹהִים "the river (or collect. the rivers) of God," i. e. watering the city [Thes. "the land"] with the blessing of God. Plur. פְּלָגִים Isa. 30:25; constr. פַּלְגֵי like פַּלְגֵי מַיִם Psa. 1:3; Prov. 5:16; 21:1; used of streams of tears, Lam. 3:48 (compare [Arabic] a stream of tears).
(2) [*Peleg*], pr. n. of a patriarch ("division, part"), the son of Heber, Gen. 10:25; 1 Ch. 1:19.

פְּלַג Ch. *half*, Dan. 7:25.

פֶּלֶג or פְּלַגָּה only in pl. פְּלַגּוֹת *rivers, streams*, Jud. 5:15, 16; Job 20:17.

פְּלֻגָּה f. *a division, class* (of the priests), i. q. מַחֲלֹקֶת 2 Ch. 35:5.

פְּלֻגָּה or פְּלֻגָּא Ch. id. Ezr. 6:18.

פִּלֶגֶשׁ and פִּילֶגֶשׁ with suff. פִּילַגְשׁוֹ, pl. פִּלַגְשִׁים and פִּילַגְשִׁים.
(1) f. *a concubine* (Ch. פִּלַקְתָּא, פִּילַקְתָּא id. With this accord πάλλαξ, παλλακίς, παλλακή; Lat. *pellex*. The etymology is obscure, but the origin may be sought with some appearance of truth in the idea of softness and pleasure; with the Phœnicio-Shemitic roots פלג, פלק, compare מָלַח No. I, and the observations there made; and with the Gr. παλλακίς, comp. μαλακός), Gen. 22:24; 35:22; Jud. 19:9, seqq., and frequently; more fully אִשָּׁה פִילֶגֶשׁ Jud. 19:1; 2 Sa. 15:16; 20:3.
(2) ὁ πάλλαξ, *a paramour*, i. q. מְאַהֵב Eze. 23:20, compare verse 5.

פָּלַד an unused root. Arab. [Arabic] to cut (compare the remarks at פָּלָא). Hence—

פְּלָדָה f. *iron*, Syr. [Syriac], Arab. [Arabic] iron of a finer kind, steel. Pl. פְּלָדוֹת things made of iron, perhaps scythes for war chariots, Nah. 2:4, בְּאֵשׁ פְּלָדוֹת הָרֶכֶב "the chariots (shine) with fire of irons," the chariots shine with steel, or scythes.

פִּלְדָּשׁ [*Pildash*], pr. n. m. Gen. 22:22 (the etymology is obscure).

פָּלָה not used in Kal, i. q. פָּלָא TO SEPARATE, TO DISTINGUISH.

=== RIGHT COLUMN ===

NIPHAL, *to be separated, distinguished*, followed by מִן Exod. 33:16; also *to become distinguished, admirable*, Ps. 139:14.
HIPHIL—(1) *to separate, to distinguish*, Exod. 8:18; followed by בֵּין to distinguish *between, make a distinction*, Ex. 9:4; 11:7.
(2) *to distinguish*, i. e. *to make distinguished* or *illustrious*, Ps. 4:4; 17:7.
Derivative, פְּלֹנִי.

פַּלּוּא ("distinguished"), [*Pallu*], pr. n. of a son of Reuben, Gen. 46:9; Ex. 6:14. Patron. פַּלֻּאִי Num. 26:5.

פָּלַח TO CLEAVE, specially TO FURROW the ground, Ps. 141:7. (Arab. [Arabic] id.; hence to till a field, [Arabic] a husbandman. Compare Ch. and as to the origin under the root פָּלָא.
PIEL—(1) *to cleave, pierce*, e. g. as a dart the liver, Prov. 7:23; Job 16:13; *to cut up*, e. g. gourds, 2 Ki. 4:39.
(2) *to cause* (young ones) *to cleave the womb and break forth*, i. e. *to bring forth*, Job 39:3. Compare פָּטַר.

פְּלַח Ch. *to labour*; hence *to serve* (so often in the Targg.); specially, to worship God (compare עָבַד); followed by an acc. and לְ Dan. 3:12, seq.; 7:14, 27.
Derivative, פָּלְחָן.

פֶּלַח f. (Job 41:16).—(1) *a part cut off, a slice* of an apple, or fig, Cant. 4:3; 1 Sa. 30:12.
(2) *a mill-stone*, so called from the even and cut away part, which is the lower in the upper mill-stone, the upper in the nether. Arab. [Arabic]. And the upper mill-stone is fully called פֶּלַח רֶכֶב (the rider-stone), Jud. 9:53; 2 Sam. 11:21; and simply רֶכֶב, the lower פֶּלַח תַּחְתִּית Job 41:16.

פִּלְחָא ("a slice"), [*Pileha*], pr. n. m. Neh. 10:25.

פָּלְחָן Ch. m. *worship, service* of God, Ezr. 7:19 See the root.

פָּלַט prop. TO BE SMOOTH, SLEEK, i. q. מָלַט, which see. Hence *to slip away, to escape*, Eze. 7:16 (Syr. [Syriac], Arab. [Arabic] id.); also *to cause to escape* (compare pr. n. פְּלַטְיָה).
PIEL—(1) i. q. Kal, but intens. *to slip away altogether*, Job 23:7.
