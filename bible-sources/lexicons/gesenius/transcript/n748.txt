RUNNING HEADS: קום־קוש | DCCXXIX | קום־קוש
followed by עַל and אֶל on account of any person or thing, 2 Sa. 3:33; Eze. 27:32.
Derivative, קִינָה.
קוֹם[?] see קָסַם[?].
קוֹעַ an unused root; Arab. قاع Med. Waw, Conj. I. VIII. *to cover the female*, as a male camel. Hence—
קוֹעַ Ezek. 23:23, prop. apparently, *a stallion*; hence figuratively, *a prince* (as rightly given by the Vulg. and Hebrew interpreters); a metaphor of frequent use amongst the Hebrews and Arabs, compare עַתּוּד, and Arab. قريع, قرم, فحل, all of which denote a male camel for the breeding of a noble race, and also a prince. There is added, paronomastically, שׁוֹעַ happy, rich. Others regard וְקוֹעַ and שׁוֹעַ as opposites, *high and low*, taking this from the root [Arabic], some of the derivatives of which signify *low, ignoble*.
קוּף an unused root, i. q. נָקַף No. 3, *to surround*, whence תְּקוּפָה circuit.
קוֹף m. *an ape*, 1 Ki. 10:22; Sanscr. and Malabar, *kapi*, an ape (prop. nimble), a word of Indian origin, whence the Gr. κῆπος, κῆβος, κεῖβος, words used to denote apes, and especially monkeys with tails.
קוּץ an unused root, i. q. קָצַץ *to cut off*, whence קְוֻצּוֹת.
I. קוּץ i. q. קוֹט—(1) TO BE WEARY OF any thing, TO LOATHE. (The primary signification I think to be that of *vomiting*, so that it is onomatopoetic, like the corresponding German verb, comp. קוֹא.) Followed by בְּ of pers., Lev. 20:23; Nu. 21:5.
(2) *to fear*, followed by מִפְּנֵי Ex. 1:12; Nu. 22:3; Isa. 7:16; both of these significations are also found conjoined in the verbs קָנַט[?], مل, ضجر, and German Grauen haben vor etwas.
HIPHIL הֵקִיץ *to put* a city *in fear*, i. e. to besiege it, Isa. 7:6; compare Arab. ضجر Conj. III. to cause to fear, to besiege.
II. קוּץ only in—
HIPHIL הֵקִיץ intrans. TO BE AROUSED, i. q. יָקַץ out of sleep, Ps. 3:6; 73:20; from the slumber of death, Job 14:12; Dan. 12:2. Imp. הקיצה[?] i. q. עוּרָה awake, arise (O Lord), Ps. 35:23.
=== RIGHT COLUMN ===
III. קוּץ—(1) i. q. קָצַץ TO CUT, TO CUT UP, CUT OFF. Hence קוֹץ a thorn, so called from the idea of cutting or wounding, and קַיִץ harvest, pr. the cutting off of fruits, summer. From this noun is derived—
(2) *to pass the summer*. (Arabic قاظ Med. Ye, id), Isa. 18:6. See חָרַף No. 2.
קוֹץ m.—(1) *a thorn* (from the root קוץ n. III), collect. *thorns, briers*, Genesis 3:18; Isa. 32:13. Plur. קוֹצִים Jer. 4:3.
(2) [*Koz, Coz, Hakkoz*], pr. n. m.—(a) 1 Ch. 4:8.—(b) with the art. הַקּוֹץ Ezr. 2:61; Neh. 3:4, 21; 7:63; 1 Ch. 24:10.
קְוֻצּוֹת f. plur. *locks* of hair, so called from their being cut off, Cant. 5:2, 11. (Syr. [Syriac] id. Arab. قصة fore locks. Compare Schultens, Opp. Min., p. 246.) Root קוּץ.
קוּר TO DIG, especially a well, Isa. 37:25. (Arab. قار Med. Waw, to cut out from amongst. Kindred are כּוּר which see, נָקַר.) Derivatives, מָקוֹר [קוּרִים, קוֹרָה, קִיר, קַרְקַר].
HIPHIL, *to cause to flow forth* (water), Jerem. 6:7.
PILPEL קַרְקַר *to dig under, to undermine* a wall (so the Chald.). Isaiah 22:5, by a play of words, מְקַרְקַר קִר “they undermine a wall” (Talmud. קרקורא דקיר destruction of a wall). Hence *to destroy*. Nu. 24:17, וְקַרְקַר כָּל־בְּנֵי־שֵׁת “and will destroy all the children of pride.” LXX. προνομεύσει. Vulg. *vastabit*.
קוֹרֵא see קָרָא.
קוֹרָה masc. *a beam, a joist*, prop. transverse (see קָרָה Piel), 2 Ki. 6:2, 5; Cant. 1:17. By synecd. *a house*, like the Gr. μέλαθρον, Gen. 19:8.
קוּרִים m. plur. *slender threads*, spiders’ webs, Isa. 59:5, 6. (Arab. قور a thread made of cotton. To this answers the Greek καῖρος, *licium*, the cross threads in weaving, die Kreuzfäden, whence καιρόω, καίρωσις. The etymology is rather obscure. I suppose, however, that קוּרִים is akin to the word קוֹרָה a transverse beam.)
קוֹשׁ—(1) i. q. Arab. قاس to be bent as a bow, a circle, the back, II. to bend as a bow, comp. Gr. γαῦσος, bent. Hence קֶשֶׁת, قوس a bow, and pr. n קישון
