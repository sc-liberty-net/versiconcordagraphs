RUNNING HEADS: גדרה־גוב | CLXI | גדרה־גוב[?]
גְּדֵרָה f. const. גְּדֶרֶת[?] pl. const. גִּדְרוֹת, with suff. גְּדֵרֹתָיו Ps. 89:41 (with Tzere impure comp. جَدِيرَة).
(1) *the wall* of a city, Ps. loc. cit.; more frequently *the fence* of a vineyard, Jer. 49:3; Nah. 3:17. It differs from a living hedge (מְשׂוּכָה), Isa. 5:5.
(2) *a place fortified with a wall*, and i. q. Arab. جَدِيرَة *a fold for flocks*, i. e a stall erected in the fields, open above, walled all around, fully גִּדְרוֹת צֹאן Num. 32:16, 24, 36. As to the thing, compare Hom. Od. ix. 185. Hence with art. הַגְּדֵרָה [*Gederah*], pr. n. of a town in the plain country of the tribe of Judah, Josh. 15:36, perhaps the same as is elsewhere called בֵּית גָּדֵר. ["Comp. Pun. נדר[?] i. e. *Gades* in Spain, see Monumm. Phœn. p. 304, seq.; also Γαδαρὰ a city of Peræa, Γαδαρηνὸς, Mat. 8:28."] Gent. n. is גְּדֵרָתִי 1 Ch. 12:4.
גְּדֵרוֹת ("folds"), [*Gederoth*], Josh. 15:41, and with art. הַגְּדֵרוֹת 2 Ch. 28:18; also pr. n. of a town in the tribe of Judah.
גְּדֵרוֹתַיִם ("two sheep-folds," comp. מִשְׁפְּתַיִם), [*Gederothaim*], pr. n. of a town in the plain country of the tribe of Judah, Josh. 15:36.
גְּדֵרִי [*Gederite*], Gent. n. from בֵּית־גָּדֵר or from גֶּדֶר which see. 1 Ch. 27:28.
גָּדַשׁ i. q. Chald. גְּדַשׁ TO HEAP UP, TO FILL. Hence גָּדִישׁ[?] which see.
גֵּה Eze. 47:13, an erroneous reading for זֶה as in verse 15, which is expressed in translating by the LXX., Vulg., Chald., and found in 14 MSS. Comp. under בַּ[?].
גָּהָה pr. TO THRUST AWAY, TO REMOVE, specially the bandage of a wound ["*to cure*"]. Hos. 5:13, וְלֹא־יִגְהֶה מִכֶּם מָזוֹר "he (the king of Assyria) shall not thrust away from you (the Jews) the bandage," i. e. he shall not heal you, as in the other hemistich. (Syr. [Syriac] to withdraw, to flee. Aphel, to rest, to liberate; Arab. جه[?] to repel.) The Hebrews explain גָּהָה by רפא. Hence—
גֵּהָה f. *removal of bandage*, i. e. *healing* of a wound. Prov. 17:22, לֵב שָׂמֵחַ יֵיטִיב גֵּהָה "a joyful heart gives a happy healing." LXX. εὐεκτεῖν ποιεῖ. Comp. 16:24.
גָּהַר TO BOW ONESELF DOWN, TO PROSTRATE ONESELF, TO LAY ONESELF DOWN. 2 Ki. 4:34, 35,
=== RIGHT COLUMN ===
used of Elisha in the raising of the dead child, יִגְהַר עָלָיו "and he bowed himself upon him." 1 Ki. 18:42, וַיִּגְהַר אַרְצָה "and he cast himself down on the ground." This signification, which the context almost demands, is expressed by all the ancient interpreters (except the Ch. and Arabic, 2 Ki.). The Syriac has the same word under the letters [Syriac] Ethpeal, to which answers the Ch. גְּחַן; see examples of the interchange of the letters ר and נ[?], under the letter Nun.
גַּו with suff. גַּוִּי m. *the back* (from the root גָּוָה No. I); in one phrase הִשְׁלִיךְ אַחֲרֵי גַו "to cast behind one's back," i. e. to neglect, to despise. 1 Ki. 14:9; Eze. 23:35; Neh. 9:26; comp. הִשְׁלִיךְ. The same is often used in Arabic, نبذ ورا ظهره, جعل بظهره.
גַּו Chald. const. גּוֹ and גּוֹא with suff. גַּוֵּהּ, גַּוַּהּ[?] mas. *middle, midst*, see גֵּוָה No. I. (Syr. [Syriac] id. Arab جَو inner part of a house, جُوّا within.) Hence
(a) בְּגוֹ, בְּגוֹא i. q. בְּתוֹךְ *in the midst*, and simply *in*. בְּגוֹ נוּרָא in the fire, Dan. 3:25; 4:7; 7:15. בְּגַוֵּהּ in it, Ezr. 4:15. Ezr. 6:2, בְּגַוַּהּ כְּתִיב כֵּן דִּכְרוֹנָה "so in it (the book) was written a commentary." Ezr. 5:7.—(b) לְגוֹא *into*, Dan. 3:6, 11, 15.—(c) מִן־גּוֹא "out of the midst," Dan. 3:26.
גֵּו (for גֵּוֶה, like בֵּן for בְּנֶה from the root גָּוָה No. I.), const. גֵּו; with suff. גֵּוְךָ, גֵּוִי m.
(1) *back*, Pro. 10:13; 19:29; 26:3; Isa. 50:6; 51:23; הִשְׁלִיךְ אַחֲרֵי גֵו Isa. 38:17; see under גַּו.
(2) ["prop. *belly*"], *middle, midst*; Job 30:5, מִן־גֵּו יְגֹרָשׁוּ " they are driven from among (men)."
גּוֹא see גַּו Chald.
גּוּב—(1) i. q. جاب Med. Waw and Ye; TO CLEAVE, TO CUT; whence גַּב a plank. Hence—
(2) *to dig a well*, like the Arab. Conj. VIII.; see גֵּב No. 2.
(3) i. q. יָגַב *to plough*, to cut the ground with a plough; hence 2 Ki. 25:12 (גָּבִים) גבים כתיב, *ploughmen*, in קרי, יוֹגְבִים.
גּוֹב *a locust* (from גָּבָה which see), Nah. 3:17; pl. (or collect.) גּוֹבַי and גֹּבַי (for גּוֹבִים Lehrg. p. 523), Amos 7:1; Nah. 3:17, גּוֹב גּוֹבַי "the locust of locusts," of a great abundance of them. Chald. גּוֹבָא, גּוּבָּא; pl. גּוֹבַי, גּוֹבָאֵי.
גּוֹב ("pit"), [*Gob*], pr. n. of a place otherwise unknown, where David fought with the Philistines, 2 Sa. 21:18, 19.
