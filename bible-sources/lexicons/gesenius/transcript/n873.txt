ת

*Tav* (Tau), the twenty-third [reckoning שׁ and שׂ for two letters] and last letter of the alphabet, when used as a numeral denoting *four hundred*. As to the signification of the name see under the word תָּו.
As to pronunciation, ת without Dagesh is an aspirated letter, and seems to have a lisping sound, like Gr. θ, and *th* English. When it has Dagesh lene (תּ) it is a slender *t*; as to its difference from ט, see page CCCXVI, A. To this there answers in Arabic ت, rarely ث, as in תָּקַף, ثقف. It is sometimes interchanged with Shin (p. DCCXCVII, A) and Tet (p. CCCXVI, B), and it has even some relation to the breathings (א, ה), see Hebr. Gramm. page 101, note; also, אוֹב, שׁוּב, תּוּב to return; אָוָה and תָּוָה to dwell; also, to mark out, and so often in Arabic.

תָּא m. *a chamber*, 1 Ki. 14:28; Eze. 40:7, seq. (Ch. תָּוָא, תָּוָן, Syr. [Syriac], [Syriac].) Plur. תָּאִים, once תָּאוֹת Eze. 40:12, from the root תָּוָה No. III, to dwell. The form תָּא appears to spring from תָּו (for תָּוֶה), the letter ו being changed because of the preceding Kametz into Aleph, as קָם, קָאם, קַוָם.[?]

תַּאֲוָה f. (from the root אָוָה No. I)—(1) *desire, longing*, whether good and just, Psa. 10:17; 21:3; or wicked, Ps. 112:10.
(2) in a bad sense, *lust, desire* (Lust, Gelüst). Nu. 11:4, הִתְאַוּוּ תַּאֲוָה "they lusted a l u s t." Psal. 78:29, 30. קִבְרוֹת הַתַּאֲוָה the graves of lust, Num. 11:34, 35.
(3) *delight, object of desire*. מַאֲכָל תַּאֲוָה food of delight, i. e. delicate, Job 33:20; Gen. 3:6; also, *honour, ornament*, Gen. 49:26; Prov. 19:22.

תָּאוֹם *a twin*, only plur. תְּאוֹמִים Gen. 38:27; by a Syriacism contr. תּוֹמִם Gen. 25:24; const. תְּאוֹמֵי Cant. 4:5, from the root תָּאַם.

תַּאֲלָה f. (from the root אָלָה), *curse, execration*, Lam. 3:65.

תָּאַם TO BE TWIN, DOUBLE. Part. תְּאָמִים double (used of planks or beams), Exod. 26:24; 36:29. (Syr. and Arab. to be a twin.)
HIPHIL, *to bear twins*, Cant. 4:2; 6:6.
Derivative, תָּאוֹם, and—

תְּאֹם or תֹּאַם, whence pl. תְּאָמֵי *twins*, Cant. 7:4. A monosyllabic noun, of the form גְּדֹל, גָּדֵל[?], properly an abstract, put there for a concrete.

תַּאֲנָה f. *coitus*, from the root אָנָה No. 2. Piel, to cause to meet, which is applied to copulation. It is once used of the lust of the wild she-ass, Jerem. 2:24. Not less suitably, N. G. Schrœder (Observatt. ad Origg. Heb. page 10) derives the signification of lust from the root [Arabic] to be hot (compare פַּחַז).
