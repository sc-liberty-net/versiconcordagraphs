HIPHIL, intrans. *to be old, to become old* (as if to contract old age, comp. הֶחֱזִיק ["in Heb. Gr. § 52. 2, note"]), Pro. 22:6; also of plants, Job 14:8; just as Pliny applies *senesco* to trees.

זָקֵן constr. זְקַן Gen. 24:2, pl. זְקֵנִים, זִקְנֵי m. *an old man* ["either put as an adj. with a subst., as אֲבִיכֶם הַזָּקֵן 'the old man your father,' Gen. 43:27, or alone as a subst., as Gen. 19:4, etc." Thes.], Gen. 18:11; 19:4; 25:8; followed by מִן older *than* some one. Job 32:4, כִּי זְקֵנִים־הֵמָּה מִמֶּנּוּ לְיָמִים "for they were older than he;" זִקְנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, הָעִיר, מִצְרַיִם the elders of Israel, of the city, of Egypt, i. e. the chief men, rulers, magistrates, without reference to the idea of age; Ex. 3:16; 4:29; Deu. 19:12; 21:3, 4, 6; 22:15, 17, 18. (The use is similar of the Arab. شيخ *sheikh*, an old man, hence the captain of a tribe; and in the languages sprung from the Latin, Ital. *Signor*, French *Seigneur*, Spanish *Señor*, Engl. *Sir*, all of which are from the Latin *Senior;* as Germ. Graf, is properly i. q. grau, krau[?], grey-headed. In no language, however, does this reverence for old age appear more habitual and familiar, than in the Chinese; in which the ministers of a king, even though young, are called *great king father*, i. e. a man of very high eminence; and men of the same rank address each other "O my elder brother!") Metaph. used of an old nation, become weak, Isa. 47:6. Plur. f. זְקֵנוֹת Zec. 8:4.

זֹקֶן m. *old age*, Gen. 48:10.

זִקְנָה f. *old age*, Gen. 24:36; Psal. 71:9, 18. Metaph. of a nation, Isa. 46:4; comp. Isa. 47:6.

זְקֻנִים m. pl. id., Gen. 21:2, 7; 44:20. בֶּן־זְקֻנִים a son born in old age, Gen. 37:3. (As to denominatives of this form, see Lehrg. § 122, No. 13.)

זָקַף TO RAISE, figuratively TO COMFORT the afflicted, Ps. 145:14; 146:8. (Syr. ܙܩܦ id.)

זְקַף Ch. *to raise up, to hang*, e. g. a criminal on a stake set up. (Syr. ܙܩܦ to crucify.) Ezr. 6:11. [Note. "זְקִיף applies in this passage to the man, not to the wood."]

זָקַק—(1) *to tie fast, to bind* (Chald. זְקַק id.), whence זִקִּים and אֲזִקִּים bonds. [This meaning in Thes. is wholly excluded.]

With this signification agree σάκκος, σάκος, sackcloth, a strainer; σακκέω, σακκεύω, σακκίζω; Lat. *saccus, saccare;* Hebr. שַׂק; and the same stock is found in seihen, seigen, seigern, sickern, properly used of metals.

PIEL זִקֵּק *to refine, to purify* gold, Mal. 3:3.
PUAL, *to be refined*, used of wine, Isa. 25:6; of metal, 1 Ch. 28:18; 29:4; Ps. 12:7.

זָר *a stranger, an enemy;* see the root זוּר No. II.

זֵר m. *border, edge, wreathed work, crown* around a table, or the ark of the covenant, Ex. 25:11, 24, 25; 37:2, 11, 26. Syr. ܙܪܐ neckchain, collar. Root זָרַר No. I.

זָרָא f. for זָרָה once Nu. 11:20, *loathing.* Vulg. *nausea*, from the root זִיר[?], ذار which see.

זָרַב unused in Kal, i. q. Syr. ܙܪܒ TO MAKE NARROW. PUAL, used of rivers, בְּעֵת יְזֹרְבוּ *at the time* when *they become narrow* ["what time they flow off, they fail, i. e. when the waters flow off, the streams dry up"], Job 6:17. Rightly compared with Arabic مرزاب a narrow channel.

זְרֻבָּבֶל (probably for ["זְרוּבָבֶל 'scattered to Babylon,' or for"] זְרוּעַ בָּבֶל "born at Babylon"), pr. n. *Zerubbabel* (LXX. Ζοροβάβελ), a descendant of David, who brought back the first colony of the Jews to their own land, after the Babylonish captivity, Ezr. 2:2; 3:2; Hag. 1:1.
