RUNNING HEADS: שָׁחוֹר—שַׁחַת[?] | DCCCXIV | שָׁחוֹר—שָׁחַן

" (the king) is thy lord, הִשְׁתַּחֲוִי לוֹ render homage to him" [this king is Christ, who is to be worshipped as being God over all]. Inf. Ch. הִשְׁתַּחֲוָיָה 2 Ki. 5:18. As to מִשְׁתַּחֲוִיתֶם, see Analyt. Ind.
Derivatives, see under Kal No. 2.

שְׁחוֹר see שִׁיחוֹר.

שְׁחוֹר m. *blackness*, Lam. 4:8. Root שָׁחַר.

שְׁחוּת f. *a pit*, Prov. 28:10; from the root שָׁחָה No. 2. Compare שׁוּחַ.

שָׁחַח i. q. שׁוּחַ and שָׁחָה (compare שָׁכַךְ), pret. שַׁחוֹתִי, plur. שַׁחוּ and שָׁחֲחוּ, fut. יָשֹׁחַ.
(1) TO SINK DOWN, Hab. 3:6; used of beasts of prey couching down and lying in wait in their lairs, Job 38:40.
(2) *to be bowed down, cast down*, Psa. 10:10; 107:39; Isa. 2:11, 17; specially with sickness, Psa. 35:14; 38:7; Lam. 3:20; also, *to submit oneself.* Isa. 60:14, וְהָלְכוּ אֵלַיִךְ שְׁחוֹחַ " they shall come to thee bowing down." Job 9:13; Pro. 14:19.
NIPHAL, *to be bowed down*, Isa. 2:9; 5:15. Used of a depressed and attenuated voice, Ecc. 12:4. Pregn. Isa. 29:4, וּמֵעָפָר תִּשַּׁח אִמְרָתֵךְ " and thy depressed (slender) voice shall be heard from the dust."
HIPHIL, *to bring down, to humble*, Isaiah 25:12; 26:5.
HITHPOEL, *to be cast down* (the soul), Psa. 42:7, 12; 43:5.
Derivative, שַׁח.

שָׁחַט Arab. سحط—(1) TO SLAY animals, Gen. 37:31; Isa. 22:13; especially a victim, Levit. 1:5, 11; and even a human victim, Gen. 22:10; Isa. 57:5. Hos. 5:2, שַׁחֲטָה שֵׂטִים הֶעְמִיקוּ " in slaughtering (victims) they make deep their transgression."
(2) *to kill* (persons), 2 Ki. 25:7; Jerem. 39:6. חֵץ שׁוֹחֵט Jer. 9:7, a deadly arrow. In קרי there is שָׁחוּט, the sense not being different, prop. made to kill.
(3) זָהָב שָׁחוּט 1 Ki. 10:16, 17; 2 Chr. 9:15, 16, prob. *mixed gold*, alloyed with another metal, comp. Arab. سحط to dilute wine with water; see more in Comment. on Isaiah 1:22. The LXX. understood gold *beaten out*, compare שָׂטַח to spread out, the letters being transposed.
NIPHAL, pass. of No. 1, Levit. 6:18; Num. 11:22. Hence—

שְׁחִיטָה f. *the slaying* of victims, 2 Chr. 30:17.

שְׁחִין masc. *an inflamed ulcer, a boil* (see the root שָׁחַן) Ex. 9:9, 11; Lev. 13:18—20. שְׁחִין מִצְרַיִם

=== RIGHT COLUMN ===

the boil of Egypt, Deu. 28:27, 35; and שְׁחִין רָע Job 2:7; used of a kind of black leprosy endemic in Egypt, called by physicians *elephantiasis*, from the skin being covered with black scales, and the feet swelling up. Compare Plin. H. N., xxvi. § 5. Schilling, De Lepra, p. 184.

שָׁחִים m. Isa. 37:30, i. q. סָחִישׁ which see. This would seem to be the primitive form, prop. denoting *sprout, shoot*, comp. the root שָׁחַץ, Arab. شخص to lift oneself up, to lift the eyes, perhaps Heb. *to sprout, to shoot forth.* We may also compare יַחַשׂ prop. offspring, progeny.

שָׁחִיף or שְׁחִיף masc. *a board*, as being thin, so called from its being pared, planed away (dünngehobeltes Bret), from the root שָׁחַף, Eze. 41:16.

שְׁחִית f. *a pit*, plur. שְׁחִיתוֹת (compare Lehrgeb. p. 527) Ps. 107:20; Lam. 4:20; from the root שָׁחָה to sink down.

שְׁחִיתָה f. Chald. evil deed, see שְׁחַת.

I. שָׁחַל an unused root, Arab. سحل to peel off, to shell, to scale, whence שְׁחֵלֶת. To this answer in the Indo-Germanic languages, σκῦλον, Germ. Schale, schälen, Engl. *to scale.*

II. שָׁחַל perhaps, *to roar*, as a lion. Compare Arab. سحل to bray, as an ass, سحال *vox in pectore reciprocata*, the braying of an ass. Jeuhari and Kam.; cognate roots are צָלַל, הָלַל, German, hallen, gellen, schallen (Notk. skellen). Hence—

שַׁחַל m. *a lion*, a poetic word, Job 4:10; 10:16; 28:8; Psa. 91:13; Prov. 26:13; Hos. 5:14; 13:7. Bochart (Hieroz.) understands the blackish lion of Syria (Plin. H. N., viii. 17). Compare שָׁחֹר, the letters ל and ר being interchanged.

שְׁחֵלֶת f. Ex. 30:34; according to Hebrew interpreters, *unguis odoratus*, prop. the covering or *shell* of a kind of muscle found in the lakes of India, where the nard grows; which, when burning, emits an odour resembling musk: this is now called *blatta byzantina*, Teufelsklaue. See Dioscorid., ii. 10; and the Arabian writers in Bochart (Hieroz. ii. p. 803, seq.). The root is שָׁחַל to peel off, compare שִׁיחֲלָא[?] the pod of dates.

שָׁחַן an unused root, Arab. سخن to be hot, to be inflamed, Syriac ܫܚܢ[?] Pa. to ulcerate, whence שְׁחִין ulcer, which see.
