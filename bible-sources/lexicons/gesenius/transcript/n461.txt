RUNNING HEADS: לקח—לשון[?] | CCCCXLII | לקח—לשון
2 Sam. 4:6, "they came to fetch wheat" (um Weizen zu holen); *to bring* any thing (bringen, hin=, herbringen), Gen. 18:5, 7, 8; Job 38:20; *to bring*, as an offering (darbringen), Gen. 15:10; Ex. 25:2; 35:5.
(2) *to take*, i. e. *to receive*, empfangen (i. q. λαμβάνειν, in Passow. lett. B), Num. 23:20, "behold, I have received a blessing from God." Specially—(*a*) *to receive* (aufnehmen) for care and protection, Ps. 49:16; 73:24.—(*b*) to receive with the ears, i. e. *to perceive* (vernehmen), Job 4:12, compare לֶקַח.—(*c*) *to receive* (i. q. to admit), e. g. prayers, counsel, Ps. 6:10; Prov. 2:1; 4:10; 24:32.
NIPHAL נִלְקַח pass. of Kal 1, letter *b*, 1 Sam. 4:11, seq.; 21:7; 2 Ki. 2:9, letter *d*; Est. 2:8, 16. More frequently in the passive signification are used—
PUAL, pret. לֻקַּח and fut.—
HOPHAL יֻקַּח pass. of No. 1, *to be taken*, Gen. 3:19, 23; letter *b*, Isa. 49:24, 25; 52:5; Jud. 17:2; letter *d*, Gen. 12:15; 18:4.
HITHPAEL, part. אֵשׁ מִתְלַקַּחַת Ex. 9:24; Eze. 1:4, "a fire taking hold of itself," as if a mass of fire; see Syn. הִתְלַכֵּד.
Derivatives, מַלְקָחַיִם [מֶלְקָחַיִם], מַלְקוֹחַ, מַקָּחָה, קַחַת [?], מִקָּח, pr. n. [לִקְחִי], and—
לֶקַח m.—(1) *arts*, by which any one's mind is *captivated* (Kunst jem. einzunehmen), Prov. 7:21; compare root No. 1, letter *c*.
(2) *doctrine, knowledge*, which any one *receives*, i. e. perceives, or learns (see root No. 2, *b*; compare Syr. [Syriac] to receive and learn; Gr. παραλαμβάνω and Lat. *accipio*), Pro. 1:5; 9:9; Isa. 29:24. So far as this is communicated to others, i. q. *instruction, the discourse* of one who teaches, Prov. 4:2; Deut. 32:2; Job 11:4.
לִקְחִי ("learned," "imbued with learning"), [*Likhi*], pr. n. m. 1 Chron. 7:19.
לָקַט Kal and Piel TO GATHER, TO COLLECT, prop. things lying on the ground, e. g. ears of corn, Ruth 2:3, 7, 15; stones, Gen. 31:46; flowers, Cant. 6:2; manna, Exod. 16:4, seq. Once used of collecting money, Gen. 47:14. ["Arab. لقط to collect any thing lying on the ground; Syr. [Syriac] Peal and Pael, used of collecting wood; Sam. [Samaritan], but Nasar. [Syriac] to collect." Thes.]
PUAL, Isaiah 27:12, and—
HITHPAEL, Jud. 11:3, *to be gathered, assembled* (of persons), Arab. and Aram. id.

=== RIGHT COLUMN ===
לֶקֶט m. *a gleaning* of ears of corn, or of grapes, Lev. 19:9; 23:22.
לַקֻּם see לַקּוּם. ["Arab. لقم to stop the way."]
לָקַק fut. יָלֹק onomatopoet. TO LICK, TO LAP, used of dogs drinking by lapping, 1 Kings 21:19; 22:38; Jud. 7:5. A kindred root is לָחַךְ, which see; also page CCCCXXXIV, B. Also Armen. [Armenian] *lakiel*, is to lap; Arab. [Arabic] a tongue.
PIEL, id. Jud. 7:6, 7.
לָקֵשׁ unused in Kal; Syr. Pael TO BE RIPE, LATE (used of fruit); whence מַלְקוֹשׁ the latter rain, and לֶקֶשׁ hay of the latter growth. Hence—
PIEL, *to gather late fruit*, i. e. to glean, Job 24:6 (where some copies act as interpreters by reading ילקטו[?]).
לֶקֶשׁ m. *hay of the latter growth, aftermowth*, Am. 7:1.
לָשַׁד an unused root. Arabic لسد to suck; whence—
לְשַׁד m.—(1) *juice*, from the idea of sucking; specially *vital moisture, vigour*; Ps. 32:4, נֶהְפַּךְ לְשַׁדִּי "my vigour is changed," i. e. is dried up.
(2) *a sweet cake*; Nu. 11:8, לְשַׁד הַשָּׁמֶן "a cake of oil," an oiled cake; LXX. ἐγκρὶς ἐξ ἐλαίου.
לָשׁוֹן of both genders, but more often f.—(1) THE TONGUE of men or of animals, Ex. 11:7; and so frequently. (Arab. لسان, Æthiop. ልሳን፡ Aram. לִשָּׁן, [Syriac], also in languages not Phœnicio-Shemitic, Sanscrit *rasana*, Armen. [Armenian] *liezu*, Coptic ⲗⲁⲥ, and even γλῶσσα, in which γ is an addition; comp. λεύσσω, γλαύσσω, γνόφος, νέφος, χλαῖνα, *lana* and many other words. [Welsh, *llais*, voice, *lleision*, endued with voice.] The original idea is that of *lapping*, a power which is found in the syllable *las*, compare לָחַשׁ, لحس, لس, לְשַׁד. A secondary root is the Poel לוֹשֵׁן which see.) תַּחַת לְשׁוֹן פ׳ *under the tongue* of any one is used in Hebrew for *in the mouth*, Ps. 10:7; 66:17. Specially used of *a calumnious* or *malignant tongue* (what the Chaldeans and Zabians call a *third* tongue, compare Sir. 28:15; and the verb לִשֵּׁן). Ps. 140:12, אִישׁ לָשׁוֹן "a man of tongue," i. e. a slanderer (but בַּעַל לָשׁוֹן is *an enchanter*, Ecc. 10:11).
