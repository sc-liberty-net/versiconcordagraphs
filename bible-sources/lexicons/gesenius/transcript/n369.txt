HIPHIL הֵילִיל, fut. יְהֵילִיל (Isa. 52:5), יְיֵלִיל (Lehrg. p. 389).
(1) *to cry out, to lament, to howl*, Isa. 13:6; 15:3; 23:1, 14; Jer. 25:34; Am. 8:3, הֵילִילוּ שִׁירוֹת הֵיכָל "the songs of palaces shall howl" (shall become sad, shall be mournful).
(2) Once used of the joyful voices of proud conquerors, Isa. 52:5; so [Arabic] of the sound of war, Barhebr. p. 411, 413, Gr. ὀλολύζειν of a joyful outcry, Æschyl. Septem ante Theb. 831; Agam. 281; and on the contrary ἀλαλάζειν for lamentation, Eurip. Phœn. 358. Also הֵרִיעַ, רָנַן, מִרְזָח are used for outcry of either kind.—There seems to be a trace of the form הוֹלִיל (compare وَلْوَل, and the noun תּוֹלָל), Ps. 78:63, where for הוּלָּלוּ the parallelism requires us to read הוֹלִילוּ (are lamented). [Few, I believe, will approve of any such alteration; the passage needs none.]
Derivatives, תּוֹלָל and—

יְלֵל m. *yelling* or *howling* of wild beasts, Deu. 32:10; comp. Arab. يباب *yelling*, for خراب يباب a desert where wild beasts yell (Willmet's Lex. Arab. s. v.), and Hebr. אִיִּים.

יְלָלָה const. state יִלְלַת f. *howling, yelling*, Isa. 15:8; Jer. 25:36; Zec. 11:3.

יָלַע i. q. לָעָה [i. q. לוּעַ Thes.], Arab. لغى TO UTTER anything RASHLY (Syn. בָּטָא), Pro. 20:25. In Arabic the verb لغى is used of rash oaths, Kor. Sur. ii. 225. As to the power of the syllable לע see under לוּעַ.
[Hence תּוֹלָע and תּוֹלֵעָה in Thes.]

יָלַף an unused root. Arabic [Arabic], Conj. III. to adhere firmly. Hence—

יַלֶּפֶת f. *a scab, an* itching *scab*, so called from sticking fast, Levit. 21:20; 22:22; LXX. λειχήν, Vulg. *impetigo*.

יָלַק an unused root, i. q. לָקַק *to lick, to lap*, to lick up, to browse upon, like cattle (comp. לָחַךְ Nu. 22:4). Hence—

יֶלֶק m. a species of *locust*, with wings (Nah. 3:16) and hairy (Jer. 51:27), Ps. 105:34; Joel 1:4; 2:25; see Bochart, Hieroz. P. ii. p. 443.

יַלְקוּט *a bag, a purse*, so called from collecting (root לָקַט), 1 Sa. 17:40.

=== RIGHT COLUMN ===
יָם const. יַם־, more rarely יָם־; with suff. יַמּוֹ Jer. 51:36; with ה parag. יָמָּה; pl. יַמִּים m.—
(1) THE SEA (Arab. يم, Syr. [Syriac] and [Syriac] but the latter word is generally used for a lake; Ægypt. ⲓⲟⲙ[?] id. A derivation is scarcely to be sought; however it may be conjectured that it properly denotes, *the boiling, foaming* of the sea; compare יוֹם[?] and יָמִים; [referred in Thes. to the unused root יָמַם]). Used either of the ocean or any of its parts, or of inland lakes (Job 14:11, Syr. [Syriac]). So יָם־כִּנֶּרֶת the lake of Tiberias, Nu. 34:11; יָם־הַמֶּלַח Gen. 14:3 (the salt sea), יָם הָעֲרָבָה (sea of the desert) Deu. 4:49, יָם הַקַּדְמֹנִי (eastern sea) Joel 2:20; Zec. 14:8, *lacus Asphaltites*, or the Dead Sea; יַם־סוּף (the weedy sea, see סוּף) and יָם־מִצְרַיִם Isa. 11:15, the Red Sea; הַיָּם הַגָּדוֹל the great sea, Nu. 34:6, 7, and הַיָּם הָאַחֲרוֹן the hinder, i. e. the western sea, Deu. 11:24, for the Mediterranean sea; הַיָּם with art. used (as shewn by the context) of the Mediterranean sea, Josh. 15:47; the sea of Galilee, Isa. 8:23; the Red Sea, Isa. 10:26; the Dead Sea, Isa. 16:8; הֲמוֹן יָם wealth of the sea, is the wealth of maritime and trans-marine nations (הָאִיִּים) procured by sea voyages; Isa. 60:5, i. q. שֶׁפַע יַמִּים Deu. 33:19; Job 7:12, הֲיָם אֲנִי אִם תַּנִּין כִּי וגו' "am I a sea, or a seamonster, that," etc., i. e. am I untamed like the sea? Lam. 2:13, גָּדוֹל כַּיָּם שִׁבְרֵךְ "thy ruin is great like the sea." Plur. יַמִּים *seas*, poet. for the sing., e. g. for the prose חוֹל הַיָּם Gen. 32:13; 41:49; poet. is said חוֹל יַמִּים Gen. 49:13; Job 6:3; Jud. 5:17; Ps. 78:27. Figuratively and hyperbolically יָם־הַנְּחשֶׁת *the sea of brass* was the name of the great laver in the inner court of Solomon's temple, 2 Ki. 25:13; 1 Ch. 18:8. The name of *sea* is also applied to—
(2) *a great river*, as the Nile, Isa. 19:5; Nahum 3:8; the Euphrates, Isa. 27:1; Jer. 51:36; pl. of the arms of the Nile, Eze. 32:2. (So Arab. يم and بحر[?] compare Diod. i. 12, 96. More instances have been given on Isa. 19:5.)
(3) *the west, the western quarter*, from the Mediterranean sea being situated to the west of Palestine; רוּחַ יָם the west wind, Exod. 10:19; פְּאַת־יָם the west side, Ex. 27:12; 38:12; יָמָּה westward, Gen. 28:14; Exod. 26:22 (also to the sea, Ex. 10:19, for which there is more frequently הַיָּמָּה); מִיָּם from the west, Gen. 12:8; מִיָּם לְ on the west of any place, Josh. 8:9, 12, 13.
