About This Concordagraph

How To Read It

Every dot is a passage; every arc is a claim that two passages are connected. An arc says two things about itself, and both are visible without clicking.

What kind of link. A solid line means the two passages share wording. A dashed line means one of them cites the other. A dotted line means they make the same move in different words.

How much weight the text puts behind it. Green means the text says so outright. Amber means it is a reasonable reading, but inferred. Grey means it was constructed by a reader and the text does not link them.

That last distinction is the point of the whole exercise. A graph that draws every connection the same way launders one reader's speculation into settled reference, and there is no way to tell afterwards which was which.

Groups & Motifs Are Different Axes

A group is a topic, and a passage belongs to exactly one. A motif is a Hebrew or Greek word, and a passage carries as many as it has words — so a motif cuts across groups by construction. The number beside each motif is how many verses in the whole Bible use that word: one shared with four verses is evidence, one shared with four hundred is vocabulary.

The Versiconcordagraphs Project

A concordagraph is a traversable network of source references in which the nodes are locatable citations and every edge carries both a type and a warrant. This page is one of them. Each topic lives on its own branch of the repository, with its own passages, its own connections and its own built page; a branch is not a version of the same work.

Every reference here resolves against a verse index built and checked locally. The build refuses to write the page if a reference does not resolve, an edge points at a missing passage, or a warrant tier is misspelled.

No Copyrighted Scripture Text

These pages carry verse references and summaries written for this project, never the wording of a copyrighted translation. Where the Word Study is present, a passage also shows this project's own literal English — a draft, not yet reviewed — and the King James Version, which is public domain; its sources and licences are credited beneath each word's study. The index itself is never published. Each reference links out to Bible Hub, where every major translation of that verse sits side by side, and to Bible Gateway for the chapter in full.
